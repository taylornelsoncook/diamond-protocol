# Legal documents: drafts for the lawyer

These are **drafts** written for Diamond Protocol to hand to a Colorado lawyer. They are not legal advice and have not been reviewed by one. Have them reviewed before publishing them; the Settings page keeps the placeholders (which start with `[`) until the owner replaces them, so nothing is shown to families by accident.

| File | What it is | Where it goes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `waiver.txt` | Participation agreement: assumption of risk, release of liability (business, owners, coaches, contractors, volunteers), indemnification, medical consent and emergency treatment, concussion acknowledgment (Colorado's Jake Snakenberg Act), photo and video policy (coaching clips private, marketing use opt-in), conduct, sites we don't own, transport, illness, electronic signature, Colorado parental release (C.R.S. § 13-22-107) | Settings → Policies → Waiver. Families sign it once in the portal; one signature covers every athlete and also records acceptance of the terms and privacy policy. |
| `terms.txt` | Terms of service: accounts, booking and late cancels, memberships billed on the 1st with Colorado automatic-renewal disclosures, the 14-day cancellation policy and training credit, sales tax, card processing fee limits, cards held by Stripe, transaction records kept 5 years, the app, conduct, teams, your data, IP, disclaimers, limitation of liability, arbitration in Douglas County, changes | Settings → Terms of service. Published at `/terms`. |
| `privacy.txt` | Privacy policy: children's privacy (COPPA), what we collect (including wearables and clips), why, who sees it (family, staff by role, teammates, schools, service providers), never sold, best-interest use, permission before sharing, retention (clips, 5-year financial records, signed agreements), rights (download, correct, delete, Colorado Privacy Act appeal), security, texts | Settings → Privacy policy. Published at `/privacy`. |
| `card-consent.txt` | The short consent sentences shown wherever a card is saved or charged | Built into the screens; reviewed here. |

`complete-agreement.txt` is the four of them in one document (waiver, terms, privacy policy, card-consent wording) for reading or sending as a whole; the app still keeps them as separate boxes, because the terms and privacy policy are also public pages, and signing the waiver records agreement to all of them at once.

## How to put them in the app

On Settings, each of the three text boxes has a **Use the drafted wording** button. It fills the box with the draft, with the business name, address, contact email, late-cancel window and web address filled in from your settings. Read it, change what the lawyer tells you to, then **Save**. Saving the waiver asks every family to sign the new version before their next booking; saving the terms or privacy policy asks every account holder to accept the new version.

## Decisions the drafts make (change them if you want)

- **14 days**: cancel any purchase within 14 days for a refund of the unused part, or the full price as training credit; camps and clinics until 14 days before the start. After that, credit only, for unused packs, a documented medical reason or a move, and camp spots. Training credit lasts 12 months.
- **Session packs expire** 12 months after purchase (the owner's "private training has a year expiration"; the app's setting Billing → Session packs expire, default 12 months, applies to every pack type). A session past its date is lost.
- **Membership sessions don't carry over**: what is left of a paid month is lost unless the owner agrees in writing to carry a stated number over (the app records it on the client page: Carry sessions over).
- **Membership cancellation** takes effect at the end of the paid month; no refund of the current month.
- **Price changes** with 30 days' notice by email.
- **Transaction records** kept at least 5 years, kept without names after an account is deleted.
- **Photos and video**: coaching clips private; any marketing use needs separate written permission the parent can withdraw.
- **Disputes**: individual arbitration in Douglas County, Colorado (American Arbitration Association), small-claims carve-out, class-action waiver. Ask the lawyer whether to keep the arbitration clause: it protects the business but some owners prefer plain court venue.
- **Limitation of liability**: what you paid in the last 12 months.
- **Insurance is the athlete's** (owner's decision): the business provides no health, accident or other insurance for the athlete; the waiver's release section says insurance covering the athlete's training is the family's responsibility, that the athlete is covered by the family's health insurance or the family accepts every cost, and that no Released Party pays medical, hospital, ambulance or rehabilitation costs. The medical consent section says the same. The lawyer should confirm the wording sits well with the release and the parental release under C.R.S. § 13-22-107.
- **Contact for everything**: the business email from your email settings.

## Colorado points for the lawyer to confirm

- Parental release of a minor's negligence claims is enforceable in Colorado under C.R.S. § 13-22-107 when the parent's decision is informed and voluntary; the waiver says so in plain words. Gross negligence and willful conduct can't be released and the waiver says that too.
- Concussions: the Jake Snakenberg Youth Concussion Act (C.R.S. § 25-43-101 et seq.) requires annual concussion training for coaches of youth athletes aged 11 to 18, removal on suspicion and written clearance to return. The waiver tells parents this; the owner should keep each coach's training certificate on file.
- Automatic renewal: C.R.S. § 6-1-732 requires clear disclosure of the renewal terms before purchase, affirmative consent, an acknowledgment with cancellation instructions, and online cancellation for memberships started online. The app shows the plan, price, class allowance, next charge date and the Charge now tick before the first charge; cancellation is in the portal. The terms describe all of it.
- Card surcharges: C.R.S. § 5-2-212 caps a surcharge at 2% (or actual cost) on credit cards only, with notice before payment; never on debit cards. The terms say the fee is shown first, never on debit, cash or checks, and within the law. The app asks Stripe whether each card is credit, debit or prepaid: a card on file that is debit or prepaid is never charged the fee, and a card that is only known once it is tapped or typed on the pay page has the fee refunded automatically if it turns out to be debit or prepaid. The app's fee setting should stay at or under 2% with no flat fee, or off.
- Sales tax: training services are generally not taxable in Colorado; tangible goods are (state plus local). The terms say tax is added where required.
- Children's data: COPPA applies to collection from children under 13; here parents create accounts and provide the data. The Colorado Privacy Act's thresholds likely don't apply to a business this size, but the drafts honor its rights anyway.
- Electronic signatures: the federal E-SIGN Act and Colorado's Uniform Electronic Transactions Act (C.R.S. § 24-71.3-101 et seq.).
