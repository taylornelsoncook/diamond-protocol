Sprint positions: the angles that make athletes fast

Coach's reference · October 2026 · the positions, angles and cues behind the Sprint tab's grades

The positions that are most effective, efficient and repeatable for max velocity, acceleration and a planted cut, with the joint angles and timing behind each. Angles read as the app reads them: thigh, trunk and shin from vertical; knees as the inside angle (180 = straight). Published values are marked apart from coaching estimates in the last table.

1. Max velocity (the 30 to 40 yard clip)

At top speed the fastest athletes do not swing their legs faster; they hit the ground harder in less time, with the foot landing under the hip and already moving backward. Contact time is the best single predictor of top speed, in youth too.

ALTIS gives the frames on purpose without angles, so the thigh angles at strike and toe-off and the take-off knee are measured values, while the MVP knee, strike separation and full-support recovery thigh are inferences from the kinograms. Front-side mechanics (less leg behind, more in front) is supported by the 2025 thigh data, but one 2017 reanalysis found the opposite sign on several of Mann's variables, so treat it as a strong coaching rule rather than a law.

2. Acceleration (the first 10 yards)

In the first steps the whole body has to sit on one forward line through the foot: the ground force points where the body leans (r = 0.93 between force direction and lean at toe-off), and trunk lean plus thigh separation at take-off explain 89% of first-step power in world-class men. Better accelerators hold the lean longer and rise toward vertical more slowly.

A 0 to 10 yard clip usually shows steps 2 to 5 rather than the first step, so the app's acceleration references sit on the step 3 to 5 values, not the block exit.

3. Change of direction (a planted cut)

The sharper the cut, the more braking has to happen before the plant: above about 60° the penultimate step is the brake and the plant is for redirection and push. The 90° cut loads the knee most, so it is the one to coach most carefully. The fastest technique (wide plant, stiff knee, big sideways push) is also the most loading, so the app grades the safety measures first.

The one number with injury evidence behind it is knee flexion at the plant: ACL injuries on video happen at 9 to 23° of knee flexion at contact (mean 23°), with valgus and hip internal rotation of 18 to 39° in the first 40 ms. Training the plant foot nearer the midline with a more upright trunk cut the valgus moment by 36%, and a six-week technique program in youth soccer improved cut time and reduced these faults at the same time. Plant contact times for adults, shorter being faster once braking has happened earlier: about 0.20 to 0.25 s for a 45° cut, 0.25 to 0.32 s for 90° and 0.35 to 0.45 s for a 180° turn.

6. Marking a clip in the app

  1. Film side-on at hip height, the camera still, iPhone slow motion. For distances, two cones a known distance apart in the frame.
  2. Pick the position, step to its frame (arrow keys move one frame; ¼ speed helps), press Use this frame.
  3. Tap five points: hip, then the knee and ankle of the leg named on screen, then the knee and ankle of the other leg. The shoulder is a sixth, optional tap for trunk lean.
  4. Save. The angles, each grade and the cue for anything off show at once; the rep's grade is the average of its positions.
  5. Send the analysis to the athlete with a note. They see the grades, the kinogram and the cues in their app.
